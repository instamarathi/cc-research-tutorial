# Exercises

## Exercise 1: Extract Your Style Guide

**Time: 20 minutes**

**What you need:** 2–3 paragraphs from your own writing that you feel represent how you actually write. Unedited work is best — something you revised until it felt right, not something a co-author or supervisor rewrote heavily. A methods section, a discussion paragraph, or a grant narrative passage all work well.

**Steps:**

1. Create a file called `my-writing-samples.md` in your working directory and paste your chosen paragraphs into it. Label each one briefly (e.g., "From methods section, paper 1") so CC has context.

2. Open CC and run:
   ```
   Read my-writing-samples.md. Analyze my writing style and produce a style guide.
   Include these sections:
   - Sentence length and variation (with examples)
   - Connective words I use (with examples)
   - How I introduce evidence (with examples)
   - How I hedge claims (with examples)
   - Paragraph structure (how I open and close paragraphs)
   - Distinctive vocabulary
   Save the style guide to style-guide.md.
   ```

3. Open `style-guide.md` and read it carefully. Ask yourself: does this sound like an accurate description of how I write? What is right? What is missing or wrong?

4. Edit `style-guide.md` directly — add anything CC missed, remove anything that does not fit, and add a "Things to avoid" section with 2–3 of your known bad habits.

**What to notice:** The gap between CC's initial description and your own self-perception is revealing. Most writers underestimate how consistent their patterns actually are — CC often identifies rhythms you did not know you had.

## Exercise 2: Write a Paragraph With Your Style Guide

**Time: 15 minutes**

**What you need:** A completed `style-guide.md` from Exercise 1, and a topic from your current research.

**Steps:**

1. Choose a topic sentence and write 3–4 bullet points of content — the facts, evidence, or ideas you want the paragraph to contain. Do not write the paragraph itself; just supply the raw material.

2. Ask CC:
   ```
   Read style-guide.md. Write a paragraph from the following content in my style:

   Topic sentence: [your topic sentence]
   - [bullet 1]
   - [bullet 2]
   - [bullet 3]
   ```

3. Read the output. Compare it to your writing samples in `my-writing-samples.md`. Do the sentences feel similar? Does CC use your connectives? Does it hedge the way you hedge?

4. If something feels off, push back: "That sentence is too long for my style — try a shorter construction." or "I would not use 'furthermore' here — what would fit better based on my style guide?"

**What to notice:** The comparison between CC's paragraph and your original samples is the test. You are looking for family resemblance, not identity — the paragraph should feel like something you could have written, not a copy of your samples.

## Exercise 3: Score Your Own Draft

**Time: 10 minutes**

**What you need:** A paragraph you wrote yourself without CC — something recent from a paper, proposal, or report.

**Steps:**

1. Paste your paragraph into CC and ask:
   ```
   Read style-guide.md. I'm going to give you a paragraph I wrote.
   Score it 0–10 for adherence to my style guide.
   List what is consistent with my style and what deviates from it, with specific references
   to the style guide.

   [your paragraph]
   ```

2. Read the scoring carefully. Where does CC say you deviated from your own style guide?

3. Ask CC to explain the biggest deviation in more detail, then ask: "Does that deviation weaken the paragraph, or is it a deliberate choice?"

**What to notice:** Sometimes CC will flag a deviation that was intentional — you broke from your usual rhythm for emphasis, or you used an unfamiliar hedge because the claim genuinely required more caution. That is fine. The score is a tool for reflection, not a grade. What matters is whether the deviations are choices or accidents.
