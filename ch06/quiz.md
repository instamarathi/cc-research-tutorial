## Q1

What is the most reliable signal that a passage of academic prose was AI-generated?

- [ ] It uses the passive voice
- [ ] It is longer than 500 words
- [x] It combines hollow transitions with uniform paragraph structure throughout
- [ ] It contains no direct quotations

> Passive voice and length are not reliable signals — academic writing routinely uses both. The reliable tell is consistency: hollow filler phrases ("it is worth noting that", "furthermore", "this highlights the importance of") combined with the same topic-sentence → three-supports → clincher structure repeating across every paragraph. Human writing is uneven; AI writing is smooth.

## Q2

You remove "It is worth noting that" from a sentence. What should you do next?

- [ ] Leave the remaining sentence as-is — the phrase was the only problem
- [ ] Replace it with "Notably," to preserve the signal
- [x] Check whether the sentence has substantive content, and either rewrite the opening to be specific or delete the sentence if it was empty
- [ ] Add a citation to fill the gap

> Removing a hollow opener is only the first step. The opener was hiding something — either a real point stated vaguely, or nothing at all. "It is worth noting that replication rates have declined" becomes either a specific claim with numbers, or it gets cut. Substituting "Notably," preserves the emptiness with different words.

## Q3

What is the correct order for the four polish passes?

- [ ] Style guide → sentence length → transitions → read aloud
- [ ] Read aloud → transitions → sentence length → style guide
- [ ] Sentence length → transitions → style guide → read aloud
- [x] Transitions first, then sentence length variation, then style guide, then read aloud

> The order matters because each pass builds on the previous. Removing hollow transitions first reveals the actual sentence structure, so the length-variation pass has cleaner material to work with. Applying the style guide after structural edits avoids fighting the style guide against the transitions. Reading aloud comes last because it catches problems introduced by all the previous passes — including new awkwardness the rewrites created.

## Think

Read this sentence and identify every AI writing marker in it. Then rewrite it as a specific, confident researcher would.

> "It is important to note that the results of the current study suggest that there may be a relationship between the variables examined, which could potentially have implications for future research in this area."

<answer>
**Markers:**

- "It is important to note that" — hollow opener; everything that follows should be important or it shouldn't be in the paper
- "suggest that there may be" — double hedge; "suggest" is already tentative, "may be" compounds it
- "could potentially" — double hedge; "could" and "potentially" both soften the same claim
- "implications for future research in this area" — vague closer; every finding has implications for future research; this says nothing

**Rewrite:** "The data show a positive correlation between [Variable A] and [Variable B] (r = 0.43, p < 0.01), which challenges [Specific Prior Theory]'s assumption that the two are independent."

The rewrite is shorter, makes a specific directional claim, states the effect size, and names the existing theory it bears on. A reader who disagrees now knows exactly what to argue with.
</answer>
