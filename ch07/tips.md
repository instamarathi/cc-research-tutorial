# Tips: Research Habits That Compound

1. **Ask CC to write your first CLAUDE.md.** Navigate to your project root and run: "Look at the files in this directory. Write a CLAUDE.md describing this project: purpose, file structure, current status." It will produce a solid draft in under a minute. Your job is to verify and add what it missed.

2. **Update CLAUDE.md after every significant session.** End a session with: "Update the Current Status section to reflect what we did today." This takes 30 seconds and saves 5 minutes of re-explanation at the start of your next session. Compounded over a semester, that is hours returned to research.

3. **Use CLAUDE.md to encode hard-won knowledge.** When you discover a quirk in your data or a caveat in your methods, record it immediately: "Note in CLAUDE.md: the sensor data has a 3-hour lag that must be corrected before analysis." Future-you will not have to rediscover it, and CC will not make the mistake twice.

4. **Keep a QUESTIONS.md alongside CLAUDE.md.** Maintain a running list of unanswered questions — methodological uncertainties, things to verify in the literature, reviewer concerns. Start sessions with: "Read QUESTIONS.md. Let's work on the first unanswered question." This turns vague research anxiety into a concrete daily agenda.

5. **Use `claude --continue` at the start of each day.** This flag resumes your last session and preserves the conversation context — not just the project context from CLAUDE.md but the thread of reasoning from yesterday's work. When you are deep in a multi-day analysis, `--continue` means CC remembers the logic you built, not just the files.

6. **Review CLAUDE.md monthly.** Remove sections that describe completed work, update conventions that have evolved, and add knowledge you have accumulated. A CLAUDE.md untouched in three months is probably out of date — it will give CC stale priorities and outdated assumptions. Treat the monthly review as a five-minute ritual at the start of a new phase of your project.
